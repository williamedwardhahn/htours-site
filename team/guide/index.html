Pepsi & Palaces

90 minutes 1.3 miles, flat, paved 1–8 guests $39 per guest
0:00 12 min

The Pharmacy Counter

Birthplace of Pepsi, 256 Middle St

Names, ground rules, restrooms. Then Caleb Bradham — a pharmacist who was not trying to build a global brand, just trying to sell a fountain drink that settled the stomach. "Brad's Drink," renamed Pepsi-Cola in 1898.

RememberAsk who has had a Pepsi in the last week. Most hands. "You are standing where it started."

0:12 6 min

The Bradham Marker

Middle & Pollock

Finish the Pepsi arc outdoors: hundreds of franchises, then a catastrophic bet on sugar after the First World War, then bankruptcy. The company that became a household name went broke here first.

RememberOverflow spot — use this when the store is packed and you cannot hold a group inside.

0:18 8 min

Middle Street

The 200 block

The commercial spine. Read the buildings — what survived, what was rebuilt, and why the rooflines change mid-block.

RememberPivot line — "Pepsi is 1898. This town was already 188 years old by then."

0:26 12 min

Christ Church

Pollock Street, churchyard

The colonial parish and its burial ground. Silver communion service given by King George II. The oldest continuous thread in town.

RememberBest stop for shade and the first place to sit if someone is struggling.

0:38 5 min

The Baxter Clock

Pollock & Middle

Short stop, one story. Something to photograph while you talk.

RememberPhoto break. Offer to take the group shot — it becomes the review photo.

0:43 14 min

Tryon Palace Grounds

Exterior only — no admission included

North Carolina's first capital. Royal governor William Tryon's residence, finished 1770, burned 1798, reconstructed in the 1950s from the original drawings. Say plainly that what they see is a reconstruction — guests respect the honesty and it makes the story better.

RememberState clearly that admission is not included, and that they should come back for it.

0:57 8 min

Pollock Street Homes

Walking east

Over 160 historic structures downtown. Pick three houses and know them cold rather than gesturing at all of them.

RememberWalking stretch — this is where you ask guests where they are from and adjust the ending.

1:05 10 min

The Fire Line

Approaching the water

The Great Fire of 1922 took roughly a thousand buildings in a day and fell hardest on New Bern's Black neighborhoods. This is why the historic district ends where it does.

RememberThe emotional turn of the tour. Slow down. Do not rush into the closer.

1:15 15 min

Where the Rivers Meet

Union Point Park

The Trent meets the Neuse. Why the town exists at this exact spot, what moved on this water, and what still does. Close with the three things you want them to remember.

RememberCloser — three facts, then restaurant recommendations, then ask for the review while you are still standing together. Never after they walk away.
